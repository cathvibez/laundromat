Special item cards

Twenty cards, seven kinds, one shared deck. You get them by rolling a 5.

Drawing

Draw two, keep one, put the other on the bottom of the deck. The card you keep sits face up in front of you — it is fresh, and cannot be played today. At the end of the day it becomes ready and joins your hand.

Playing

On your turn, before you load, you may play one ready card. Coin and Snacc happen at once. The other five attach to a washer, do their job once when it runs, then go back into the deck and are shuffled in. A card at a washer that never runs is wasted.

Coloring

Coloring

Play on a washer. When it runs, every other player's laundry inside is ruined and goes back.

Your own laundry is not ruined — but nothing else protects it either.

Coloring attaches to a washer and waits there until it runs. Every item belonging to a player other than you is then sent back, whatever the ladder said. Your own items are untouched by Coloring, but they still have to survive the ladder and the filters like everyone else's — Coloring is not armour. If two players both play Coloring on the same washer, each ruins the other, and only a player holding a Color catcher gets anything out of it.

Color catcher

Color catcher

Play on a washer. Your laundry inside is safe from Coloring.

Protects you only, and only from Coloring.

Color catcher cancels Coloring for your items in that washer — including a Coloring played after yours, and two Colorings at once. It protects nobody else, and it does nothing against the ladder, crowding, underwear, blankets or shoes. If no Coloring is ever played there, the card is wasted.

Bleach

Bleach

Play on a washer. Until it runs, every item inside counts as the opposite shade: dark counts as light, light counts as dark.

Shoes are still shoes. Bleach swaps shades, nothing else.

Bleach flips the shade of everything in the washer — including your own laundry — before the ladder is read. It does not disarm shoes: dark shoes become light shoes, which are still shoes and still beat every non-shoe item in the washer. Two Bleaches on the same washer do not cancel out; the swap happens once.

Provisional — the wording, not the rule
The brief's older shorthand — “light items wash, dark items are sent back” — gives the wrong answer whenever shoes are present. The wording above is the swap the rule actually is, and the brief has been corrected. Nothing to decide but whether you like the sentence.

Mesh bag

Mesh bag provisional

Play on a washer as you load. Everything you put into that washer this turn goes in the bag, and all of it washes when the washer runs — whatever else is inside.

Only what you load this turn, only into this washer. Not what was already in there.

Play the Mesh bag before you load, then load as normal. Every item you put into that one washer on that turn is bagged. When the washer runs, bagged items wash — the ladder, crowding, underwear isolation, blanket exclusivity and Coloring all lose to the bag. Items already sitting in the washer from an earlier day are not bagged, nor is anything you load elsewhere, nor is anything anyone else loads. Bagged items are still in the washer for everyone else's purposes: your bagged dark shoes wash for certain and still wreck the wash for the other players.

Provisional — three open questions on one card
The brief flags the Mesh bag as comfortably the strongest card in the game, with no counterplay; a predecessor card was cut in an earlier draft for exactly this.
  • (a) Keep, weaken, or revert? The v8 fallback text is written and intact: “Play on a washer as you load. Underwear you load into it this turn washes here even among other garments. Underwear already inside is not protected.”
  • (b) Do bagged items still set the ladder and count for crowding for everyone else? Currently yes.
The printed name is Mesh bag; both codebases still call it Wash net internally.
Sanitizer

Sanitizer

Play on a washer. Shoes count as ordinary clothes in this wash.

Helps whoever it happens to help — including your opponents.

Sanitizer attaches to a washer and switches off the two shoe rungs of the ladder. Shoes are still washed or sent back, but only on shade, exactly like a shirt or a pair of pants. It also flattens shoes on your own ladder: with a Sanitizer up, your own shoes no longer outrank your own shirt. Every other rule is untouched. It affects the whole washer, not just you, so it can hand two opponents a wash. A Sanitizer on a washer holding no shoes does nothing.

Coin

Coin

Turn any one washer on or off, right now. You do not need the key.

One use, then back to the deck. The keyholder still acts later today and can undo it.

Played on your turn like any other special item, and resolves at once: pick any washer that is not destroyed and flip its power. It is spent immediately and shuffled back into the deck — it is not kept, and it is not a second key. Because the keyholder acts after every player has taken their turn, the keyholder can switch your washer straight back.

Snacc

Snacc

Move Jimothy to any other washer. Everything he was sitting on goes back to its owners, unwashed.

Only playable while Jimothy is on the board.

Snacc lures the raccoon out of the washer he is in and into any other washer that has not been destroyed. The items he was holding hostage are released — released, not washed: they go straight back to their owners' hands. His new washer immediately becomes unusable and anything already sitting in it becomes hostage. You cannot play Snacc when Jimothy is not in play.

Provisional — how many of each
The deck is exactly 20 cards — a real manufacturing constraint, since 84 item + 4 event cards are fixed and 108 is the sheet-efficient total. The split across the seven kinds is unset. The app ships a flat 3/3/3/3/3/3/2 that its own config labels arbitrary and explicitly not a recommendation; the simulation still runs 14 cards, two of each. No count appears on any card. Circuit break is now settled, so this is blocked behind the Mesh bag decision alone.
Three points that catch people out. The Mesh bag covers only what you load this turn, into that one washer — yesterday's items are not in the bag. Bagged items are still in the washer for everyone else. And the Coin is not a second key: it is spent the moment it is played, and the keyholder acts last.

Event cards

Four cards, one of each. The first 6 rolled each day draws one; later 6s do not. At most one event happens per day, and the card is turned face up the instant it is drawn.

Gang

Gang

Choose a washer. The gang hides behind it and shoots it. It is out of the game for good, and everything inside goes back to its owners.

Leave this card on the wreck. Gang happens once per game and never returns to the deck.

The player who drew the card chooses the washer. Its contents return to their owners' hands unwashed, and the washer is destroyed: it can never be loaded, run, or switched on again, and the board is one washer smaller for the rest of the game. If the chosen washer is the one Jimothy is sitting in, the hostages are released as normal and Jimothy relocates to another surviving washer — the same player chooses where.

Circuit break

Circuit break

The power trips. Nothing washes tonight. Every washer keeps what is inside it, and everything is back to normal tomorrow.

Circuit break cancels tonight's reckoning and does nothing else. No washer changes power, so there is nothing to switch back on and no recovery to manage — the next night runs exactly as it would have. Every washer keeps its contents, loading carries on as normal, Jimothy is untouched, and a destroyed washer stays destroyed. It costs everybody one night.

Settled — this was one of three
The deleted faces were V2 “all off” (every washer off, keyholder restores one per day — the face this booklet used to print) and V3 “auto-restore” (all off, all back at the end of tomorrow). The old recommendation was V3 and should not be re-cited: its smoke test had an invalid control and measured the event timing that has also been replaced.

Jimothy

Jimothy

A raccoon moves in. Choose a washer: it cannot run and cannot be loaded, and everything inside is stuck with him.

Only Snacc or Animal control move him. Leave this card on the washer.

The drawer chooses which surviving washer Jimothy settles in. That washer is out of service: it does not run at the reckoning, nothing may be loaded into it, and items already inside cannot be taken out by a roll of 4. Those items are hostages, released — unwashed — only when Jimothy leaves. Neither Gang nor Circuit break removes him; Gang destroying his washer makes him move, it does not get rid of him.

Animal control

Animal control

Jimothy is taken away. Everything stuck with him goes back to its owners, unwashed.

If Jimothy is not on the board, nothing happens.

Animal control removes the raccoon from the board at once and frees his washer for use from the next load onwards. The hostages are returned to their owners' hands, not washed — players expect this card to save their laundry and it does not. If Jimothy is not in play the card does nothing at all, which is roughly half the time.

Which cards come back. Circuit break, Animal control and Jimothy's card go back into the event deck after they have done their work — though Jimothy's card stays out on the washer while he is in play. Gang does not come back, ever.

Odds and ends

Two washers cannot affect each other. Resolve them in any order you like.

Order inside a washer never matters. There is no “first in, first out”. Every item is judged against the same contents.

A card played at a washer that never runs is wasted — off, empty or destroyed, it makes no difference. The card still returns to the deck.

Switching off a washer Jimothy is in does nothing. It already could not run, and you have spent the key on it.

Animal control does not save your laundry. Hostages come back unwashed. Every player expects otherwise once.

A Coin can be overruled. It resolves during your turn; the keyholder acts later the same day.

Everything on the floor is public. Machine contents, clean piles, fresh cards, markers, Jimothy, the wreck. Only your hand and ready cards are yours.

Nothing is ever destroyed except washers. No item card ever leaves the game once it is dealt.

Tricky reckonings

“A's dark shoes” means the item belongs to player A.

1 · Shoes beat everything, including other dark items.
A's dark shoes · B's light shirt · C's dark pants		 A's dark shoes wash. Everything else goes back. Dark shoes are the top rung. C's dark pants are on rung 3 and lose to it, even though they are dark.
2 · Your own two shirts are friends. An opponent's shirt is not.
A's dark shirt · A's light shirt · B's dark shirt		 Only A's dark shirt washes. A's light shirt is on rung 4 with B's dark shirt on rung 3 above it, so the room sends it back — your own items never protect you from someone else's. B's dark shirt clears the room, but there are now three shirts and two of them are A's, so crowding takes it. crowding provisional
3 · Underwear is the hardest thing in the game to wash.
A's dark underwear · A's dark shirt		 The shirt washes. The underwear goes back. Twice over: clothing outranks underwear on A's own ladder, and underwear only ever washes in a washer holding nothing but underwear. It is your own laundry that ruined it.
4 · Underwear alone is fine, and shade still bites across players.
A's dark underwear · A's light underwear
then: A's dark underwear · B's light underwear		 Both wash — same category, shade is irrelevant among your own items.
Only A's washes. B is another player, so the room's ladder applies and dark outranks light.
5 · Three shoes cancel each other out. provisional
A's dark shoes · B's dark shoes · C's dark shoes · D's light shirt (full)		 Nothing washes at all. All three pairs clear the room's ladder, and then crowding sends all three back. D's light shirt is not rescued: crowding sends items back, it never promotes anything.
6 · A blanket, three pairs of socks, and nobody happy. provisional
A's dark blanket · A's dark socks · B's light socks · C's dark socks (legal, and exactly full)		 Only the blanket washes. Each pair of socks sees the other two beside it, which is three socks, so crowding takes all three. The blanket has only two socks beside it, which is not a crowd, and nothing outranks it. Since no socks washed, nobody even gets a damp partial.
7 · Damp socks, and the reading that surprises people.
A's light blanket · A's dark socks		 The blanket washes and the socks stay in the washer. Both are A's, both in A's top category, nobody else in the washer — but socks never wash beside a blanket. The blanket leaves clean; the socks are still in the drum tomorrow, and wash on the first night nobody puts another blanket in with them. This holds even when the blanket does not wash: the rule is about the blanket being in there.

A's dark shoes · B's light shirt · Bleach		 A's dark shoes still wash and B's shirt still goes back. The shoes read as light shoes and the shirt reads as dark — but shoes still outrank everything that is not a shoe. Bleach changes shade, never category.
10 · Coloring, and the one player who ducks it.
A's light shirt · B's light pants · C's light hat · Coloring by A · Color catcher by B		 A and B wash. C goes back. Everything is light, so the ladder would have washed all three; Coloring ruins everyone but A, and B's catcher saves B. The catcher protects its owner and nobody else.
11 · The Mesh bag beats the ladder but does not hide from it.
A's dark shoes in a Mesh bag · B's light shirt		 A's shoes wash — they were always going to — and B's shirt still goes back. The bag guarantees its own contents and changes nothing for anyone else. The shoes are still dark shoes as far as the room is concerned.
12 · The Mesh bag does not reach backwards.
A's underwear, sitting in a switched-off washer since yesterday · B's light shirt · A plays a Mesh bag today, loads nothing, and the washer is switched back on		 Both go back. The bag covers what you load on the turn you play it. Yesterday's underwear was already in there and is not in the bag, so underwear isolation takes it as usual.
